What a 13F is, and is not — read before trusting a name on this page
  • It is old on arrival. A 13F shows positions as of quarter end, filed up to 45 days later. Freshest possible: 45 days stale. The day before the next quarter's filings land: about 135 days. Every card and row above shows both dates — the reporting period and when it was actually filed.
  • Long, US-listed equities only. No shorts, no cash, no bonds, no non-US listings. A manager hedged flat against a name renders identically to a true believer — the filing cannot tell the two apart.
  • Absence proves nothing. Positions below the reporting threshold, non-US listings, and confidential-treatment holdings are all invisible here. A ticker with no line below is not a ticker no tracked manager owns — it may simply be unseen.
  • This scores nothing. Nowhere on this site does a holder count add or subtract a point from anything. Copying what a well-known investor owns, weeks after the fact, with no idea whether they have since sold, is not a strategy this project has measured — so it makes no claim about one.
