How this is measured

The market's move is removed before anything else
A pattern that happens to fire on days the whole market rallied will show a beautiful average return and mean nothing. Every pattern here is scored against what a random stock did on exactly the same days.
And so is the stock's own violence
That still is not enough. Most of these patterns — a 3% day, a week of big moves, a new low — only ever fire in stocks that are moving hard, and stocks that move hard behave differently from the quiet majority whatever pattern they made. Measured against any stock at all, this list produced eleven "winners", every one of them a volatility pattern. So the comparison group is drawn from the same volatility bucket on the same day: what did stocks moving this much, on this day, do next? What is left after that belongs to the pattern.
The day is the unit, not the stock
Three hundred stocks firing on four days is four observations, not three hundred — they all shared those four days' weather. This is not a technicality: it is the exact error that made this framework declare a known-worthless rule significant the first time it met real data. A pattern must appear on at least {{ min_days }} separate days to be scored at all.
Testing many things is paid for
At the usual threshold, one pattern in twenty looks significant by luck alone.{% if family %} All {{ family }} measurements — every pattern at every holding period — are corrected together, so trying a second holding period does not buy a second free ticket in the same lottery, and a lucky row cannot be lifted out and shown on its own.{% endif %}
Costs come out before the verdict
A real effect smaller than the spread is a way to pay a broker. Anything that survives the statistics but not the {{ cost }}% round trip is labelled "real, too small" rather than sold as an edge.
Surviving the search is not enough either
A pattern is only "confirmed" if it also held up on the half of the history it was not chosen on. One that looked real while being searched for and then failed that check is shown as "found, did not hold up" — a different, more specific failure than never having looked promising at all.
A known-worthless rule is left in on purpose
This project's own pullback rule is in the list above. It was falsified against coin-flip entry, twice. It is kept as a control: a framework that cannot reproduce a result it already knows is wrong is not measuring anything.
