How to trade this list

  1. Risk 1% of your account on any one trade. The share counts above are worked out from that. It is the single most important number on this page — it is what makes being wrong survivable.
  2. At most eight positions open, and no more than a quarter of the book in one sector. Eight bad days in a row is bad luck; eight bad days in the same sector is one bad decision made eight times.
  3. Never add to a loser. The stop is the exit. Moving it is how a 1% loss becomes a 10% loss.
  4. Get out on whichever comes first — the stop, the {{ horizon }}-session deadline, or a report falling due.
  5. Expect to be wrong about half the time. That is normal and the plan assumes it. The account grows because the losses are capped and the sizes are right, not because the picks are clever.

What decided this, and what did not

Names are scored on four things, all of them arithmetic on measured figures: how much room a company has on its debts, how violently the share moves, how big its typical {{ horizon }}-session move is against the {{ cost }}% it costs to trade, and how much it adds to a book you already hold.
